Vante App is a set of Discord bots for running a community server, plus the dashboard at [dash.vante.app](https://dash.vante.app) where server owners manage all of them. Each bot handles one area — **Essence** for registration and roles, **Voucher** for vouchers and rewards, **Security** for protection and punishments, **Gatekeeper** for join verification, **Mainframe** for statistics and leaderboards — and every one of them is configured from the same place.

## What it does

### Protection

- Antinuke guard that punishes unauthorized deletions and permission changes, then reverts them from live snapshots
- Raid protection, a whitelist with per-action flags and daily limits, and daily server checkpoints to restore from
- Automod with strikes before action

### Moderation

- Thirteen punishment types, from warnings and timeouts to role-based mutes and quarantine, with numbered cases
- Temporary punishments that expire on time, even across restarts, and are re-applied if someone rejoins
- Appeals and ban requests reviewed from Discord or from the dashboard

### Community

- Registration, referral codes, tickets, giveaways, role menus and teams
- An economy with daily rewards, a metal market, a shop, casino games and chat games
- Activity statistics, levels and leaderboards rendered as image cards

### The dashboard

- Every setting for every bot in one place, with a quick setup flow for new servers
- Live statistics, server logs with 90 days of history, deleted messages and moderation cases
- Turkish and English, passkey sign-in, and per-member access levels for staff

## How it's built

The bots live in a Bun monorepo: a small framework of my own with file-based routing for commands, buttons and modals; features packaged as plugins; and Postgres through Drizzle, with Redis for caching and cross-process updates. Each deployment exposes a tRPC API, and the Next.js dashboard talks to it through one typed contract that is validated on both ends. The same dashboard also drives priw, a single-bot version for smaller communities where features are switched on and off as modules.

I wrote about the design in detail in [How I Structure My Discord Bots and Their Dashboard](/blog/architecture), and about the database layer in [Why I Moved From Prisma to Drizzle](/blog/drizzle).
